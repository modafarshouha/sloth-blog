*Part 3 of a 3-part series exploring AI through Middle-earth.*

In the story, the Ring was carried to Mordor by two unremarkable hobbits, not by the strongest or the cleverest. What got them there was not power but small, ordinary things: looking after each other, and eating and resting when they could[^1]. However, unlike the story, we cannot destroy this Ring in Mount Doom. AI is not going away, and most of us will carry it every day.


## Use the Ring with caution

AI clearly helps people get tasks done. But there are warning signs.

In a study with nearly 1,000 high-school maths students, those who practised with a plain GPT-4 chatbot did much better during practice, yet scored 17% worse than students who never had AI once it was taken away. A version designed as a tutor, with guardrails, largely avoided that harm[^2]. Another study, a survey of 319 knowledge workers, found that the more people trusted the AI to do a task, the less critical thinking they reported, while those more confident in their own skills reported more[^3].

Neither study proves that AI makes people "dumber". They do point the same way, though: how we use the tool matters more than whether we use it.

## How I try to carry the Ring

When writing, I create the first draft myself. AI comes in afterwards, once I have something of my own to compare it with.

Small personal messages stay personal. Writing an apology is not a productivity problem.

AI is useful as a tutor rather than an oracle: I ask it to question my draft or find holes in it, instead of saying "just do it".

If the output has a phrase I would never say, I delete it. AI output can be tempting because it sounds polished, so resisting it takes practice.

I prefer tools I could walk away from. If a tool disappears tomorrow, my work should survive it.

Every now and then I do the whole task by hand, to remember that I still can.

## A rest in the Shire

None of this is anti-technology; I use AI too. Hobbits enjoy their comforts, but carrying the Ring is different. Mostly, it comes down to remembering who is in control: you or the Ring.
