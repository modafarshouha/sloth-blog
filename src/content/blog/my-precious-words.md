*Part 1 of a 3-part series exploring AI through Middle-earth.*

You have probably read a nice, well-structured post, but somewhere in it someone "delves" into a topic, and the whole thing sounds artificial to you. Each small change in the way we write looks harmless on its own, but over time this might not be the case.

## Becoming Gollum

In *The Lord of the Rings*, the One Ring looks like a convenient trick at first: it makes you invisible[^1]. Most people who carry it do not want to be changed by it; it just keeps being useful, so they keep it close. After centuries of holding the Ring, Sméagol becomes Gollum, and "my precious" is most of what is left of the person he used to be.

AI writing tools work in a similar way: they are convenient. However, the cost does not show up in a single message; it shows up in the long-term pattern.

## It is not only a feeling

Some researchers analysed more than 15 million PubMed abstracts and found that after LLMs became widely available, certain words became far more common, such as "delves" and "underscores". Their conservative estimate is that at least 13.5% of the 2024 abstracts were processed with an LLM[^2].

What's more, in a study of more than 800,000 podcast episodes, words that ChatGPT favours (such as "delve" and "meticulous") became more frequent after its release. Even a short chat with a chatbot was enough for some people to start using its words as their own, and they kept using them after a distractor task[^3]. The AI language really does move into us. And it may not stop at language: in an experiment at one school with nearly 1,000 students, those who practised maths with a plain chatbot scored 17% lower than students who never had one once the chatbot was taken away[^4].

## You are still in there

In the novel, there are times when Sméagol shows up. In reality, after the use of "delve" peaked around mid-2024, it then dropped back, around the time it started to be attributed to AI-generated text[^3]. Once people noticed the word, they seem to have stopped using it.

We cannot destroy AI in Mount Doom, and I am not sure we should: I use it too. What we can do is ask, once in a while: *is this still how I would say it?* If you cannot tell anymore, that may be the signal.
